More sketch tools

Tools on the sketch board for the jobs that come up again and again: cutting away the parts of lines you don't need, slots and nut holes, walls, rows of holes, and construction lines. (Drawings made in another program: Import a DXF or SVG drawing.) Everything they make is an ordinary outline: change it afterwards like anything else you drew. Ctrl+Z undoes each of them.

Trim (T): cut away pieces of lines

Draw shapes over each other, then take out the pieces you don't want. Pick Trim in the top bar and move the pointer over a line: the piece up to the nearest lines that cross it turns red. Click and it is gone.

  • Pieces of outlines, unfinished lines, circles and helper lines can all be trimmed. A line that nothing crosses is taken out whole.
  • To take out whole sides instead, pick them and use Delete segment (see Change part of a finished outline).
  • When you trim a side of a closed outline, the outline opens (orange ends). Trim the matching pieces of the shape it overlaps and the loose ends meet: they join into one closed outline again.
  • Rules on a side you trim are removed (its length has changed). Rules on the other sides stay.
  • For simple overlaps, Merge touching outlines (right side) does it in one click. Trim is for when you want to choose which pieces stay.

Slot (O) and Polygon (P)

  • Slot: click the center of one round end, then the center of the other, then how wide it is. Or type: after the first click, type the length from center to center and Enter, then the width and Enter (or both at once: 30,8 + Enter). The slot keeps its shape when you drag its corners: its sides stay parallel and run smoothly into the round ends.
  • Polygon: click the center, then a corner (the polygon turns in 15° steps). Or type the size across flats and Enter: a flat side goes at the bottom. A hexagon nut that is 17 mm across flats fits a 17 mm wrench; make the hole a little bigger (17.4) for a printed nut trap. While Polygon is on, the box on the right sets how many sides (3 to 64; 6 to start).

Offset: walls and clearances

A copy of an outline that is bigger or smaller by the same distance all round. Pick the outline (Select, click inside it; Shift+click more shapes), then Offset… on the right under Picked shape.

  • Corners: Sharp keeps corners as corners. Round makes the corners that point outward into arcs, so the copy is the same distance away everywhere (best for clearance).
  • Keep it keeps the original (for a wall); Replace it leaves only the copy.
  • Closed outlines and circles only (not unfinished lines or helper lines). An inward offset bigger than the shape leaves nothing; you will be told.

Pattern: rows and circles of copies

Pick a shape (Shift+click more), then Pattern… on the right. Type how many in all (counting the original). This copies shapes inside the drawing; to repeat a whole object, use Pattern on the Object tab.

  • In a row: the step between copies left-right and up-down, in mm. For a grid, make a row, pick the whole row and make a row of that going the other way.
  • In a circle: copies turn around the center of the sketch (where the red and green lines cross). 360 spreads them evenly all the way round; a smaller angle spreads them from the original up to that angle. Draw the part around the center of the sketch to use this.

Helper line or drawn line

  • Pick a straight side (Select) and click Make helper line on the right: it becomes a dotted helper line that never becomes solid. The outline opens where it was. For a whole shape, pick it and click Make helper lines (straight sides only: helper lines can't be curved).
  • Pick a helper line and click Make it a drawn line: it becomes part of the drawing and joins lines whose ends meet it. Handy to draw construction lines first, then turn the ones you want into the outline.

Level and upright, kept

A line you draw nearly level (within 2°) goes exactly level, and keeps a Flat rule (—), so it stays level when you change the drawing later. Nearly straight up and down: exactly upright, with an Upright rule (|). Rectangles get both, so dragging a corner keeps them rectangles. The same goes for helper lines.

Want a line at a small angle? Hold Ctrl while clicking (no snapping at all), or turn Level & upright off on the right under Snap to. Remove a rule you don't want by clicking its badge. More about rules: Sketch rules.
