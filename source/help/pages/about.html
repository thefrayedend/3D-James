About 3DJames

Simple to start. Room to make something your own.

3DJames is a local 3D modeling app for makers: start with a shape or draw an outline, give it exact dimensions, and build it into something useful. The aim is to keep the welcoming, hands-on feel of simple shape editors while adding the editable steps and sketch tools that let a design grow.

Why it exists

3DJames grew out of its creator's response to Microsoft discontinuing 3D Builder. Losing that approachable tool left a gap in their own workflow: they wanted simple, local-only modeling without giving up the ability to revisit a design. Rather than accept that tradeoff, they started building the app they wanted to use.

The idea is straightforward: keep modeling approachable, keep files on your computer, and keep designs editable. This is a maker's answer to that need, not a claim that no other CAD software exists.

What you can make

  • Build with shapes: move, resize, align, merge, subtract, mirror and pattern.
  • Draw your own parts: dimensioned sketches with rules, trim, offset and patterns, from scratch, from a photo or from a DXF / SVG drawing; push up, spin around, loft and sweep. Sketch on faces and curved solids too.
  • Personalize: raised or recessed text and filled SVG logos on flat solid faces.
  • Keep control: editable History steps, variables, Undo/Redo, measurements and named project versions.
  • Make practical things: ready-made maker parts and hardware, plus imports and STL, 3MF and STEP output where supported.

Unlike a finished mesh alone, an editable .3dj project keeps the modeling steps so you can return to an earlier decision. Not every imported mesh becomes a parametric solid, and individual tools have limits explained in their Help topics.

Local first

The modeling engine, Help pictures and tutorial examples are bundled with the app. Modeling works offline without an account or uploading your designs. Projects stay in files you save and in your browser's local recovery storage. Community links leave the app only when you choose to open them.

The shared browser app is built with open-source geometry and display components, including Three.js, Replicad/OpenCascade and Manifold. A Windows desktop wrapper packages that same app. It works with a mouse, fingers or a pen (see Tablets and Apple Pencil), and a 3DJames box can serve it to every device in the house. See Licenses and components (bottom left of Help) for attribution and license details.

Learn by making

The Tutorials section includes real editable examples: a mounting plate, an embossed nameplate and a hollow mug with a rounded handle. Open a copy, inspect its History, change a dimension, and see how it was made.
