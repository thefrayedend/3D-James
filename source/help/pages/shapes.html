Add and place shapes

Most designs start with a few simple shapes: put them in, give them exact sizes, and move them where they go. Everything stays changeable later.

Add a shape

On the Insert tab: Cube, Cylinder, Pyramid, Cone, Sphere, Hexagon, Wedge, Torus, Tetrahedron, Tube and Text. Two ways, like 3D Builder:

  • Click the button: the shape goes on the floor beside what is already there.
  • Drag the button into the view and let go where you want it: on the floor, or on top of another object (it stands on the top face under the pointer). While you drag, a see-through shape shows where it will land. Let go above the toolbar, or where there is no floor, and nothing is added.

Either way the new shape is picked, so you can size it right away. On a tablet, drag the button down into the view with a finger. (A finger sliding along the toolbar sideways still scrolls it.)

  • Text is a solid word: type the text, letter height, thickness and font in Properties. (To put letters onto the face of another object, use Emboss.)
  • For screws, nuts, bearings, holes and other hardware, use Parts. To draw a shape of your own, use Sketch.
  • Add (Insert tab, also ☰ > Insert a model…) brings a model from a file into this project: STL, STEP, 3MF or OBJ. STEP files come in as real CAD solids; STL, 3MF and OBJ come in as meshes, which can be moved, sized and merged but have no faces or edges to round, hollow or emboss.

Exact sizes: Properties

Pick a shape and open Properties (right). Size lists its own numbers (width, depth and height of a cube, diameter of a cylinder, number of sides of a hexagon…). Type a number and press Enter, or drag the slider next to it and watch the shape change. Any number box also takes a sum like 40+2*3, or a name from Variables.

Placement sets where its middle is: X, Y and Z are the middle of the object (a 40 mm cube standing on the floor shows Z = 20), and how it is turned. For objects made in several steps, Properties shows the overall size and volume, and Turn 90° buttons.

Move it

  • Click an object to pick it, then drag it to slide it on the floor.
  • The arrows move it in one direction only. There is one on every side, fixed to the object like real handles: left, right, front and back stick out from the middle of each side at the object's middle height, one stands above it and one below it for up-down. They stay put when you turn the view. Zoomed out, they keep a size you can grab and move out just enough to stay clear of the object, still centered on their side (like 3D Builder). One you couldn't see from where you are is hidden: behind the object, under the floor (the bottom one, unless you look from below), or pointing straight at you. Grab whichever is closest. Or hold Shift while dragging the object to lift it up or down.
  • While dragging, it snaps: flush against other objects, lined up with their edges and middles (a dotted purple line shows it), and onto the middle of holes. Hold Alt to move freely.
  • Arrow keys nudge it (Shift = 10×), Page Up / Page Down move it up and down. D or Settle (Object tab) puts it down on the floor.
  • How far a drag or nudge jumps: View > Snap (off, 0.1 to 10 mm).

Size it and turn it

  • The buttons at the left of the bottom bar switch the handles between Move, Turn and Size (keys M, R, S).
  • Drag the size arrows or corner dots, or type exact numbers in the bottom bar. The lock in the bottom bar keeps the proportions while sizing.
  • Turning snaps to steps set in View > Turn (free, 5°, 15°, 45°, 90°).
  • A shape turned on its side (or a sketch standing on an upright face) keeps its own numbers when you size it: the X, Y or Z that runs along its height changes its Height, and it grows away from its base, just like changing Height in Properties. It stays a shape you can change, not a mesh. (Only a shape turned at an angle that isn't a quarter turn becomes a stretched copy.)

Exact gaps: the Ruler

View > Ruler shows the gaps around the picked object. Click a number and type the gap you want: the object moves so it is exactly that far away. See Measure and Ruler.
