Sketch on a face or curved surface

Draw right on an object: a pocket in the top of a box, a boss on its side, a hole in the side of a cylinder. The sketch board works as always; only where the drawing sits is different.

On a flat face

  1. Insert > Sketch > On a surface…, and click a flat face of an object.
  2. The board opens on that face. Its edges show as dashed lines, and your objects seen straight on show in grey: your lines snap to their corners, middles and circle centers.
  3. Draw the outline, click Done, and choose Push up with New object (a boss standing on the face) or Cut into the face (a pocket as deep as the height). See Make the sketch solid.

On a mesh (an STL, OBJ or 3MF)

An imported mesh has no faces, only triangles, but you can still sketch on it. On a surface… finds the flat area around the spot you click: triangles that lie in one plane count as one face and light up together.

  • A flat area (the top of a box, a flat side): the board opens on it, with its outline (and the outline of any hole in it) as dashed lines to snap to, just like a solid's face.
  • A curved area (the side of a round knob): a mesh only has small flat pieces there, so you get a plane touching the mesh at the spot you clicked, with Offset and Flip, as on a curved solid below.
  • Then draw, click Done, and choose Cut into the face or New object as usual. The sketch stays editable; the mesh itself still can't be changed (for that, see Make an STL editable).

For simple pockets and bosses (rectangle, circle, slot, polygon) you don't need a sketch: Object > Faces > Shape on a face (see Edges, faces, split and emboss).

Sketch planes and the Workplane

  • Workplane (Insert tab): click a face, and new shapes and sketches go onto that face until you click Workplane again to return to the floor.
  • Insert > Sketch > Place sketch plane…: click a spot, set the plane up (offset, flip), and keep it for several sketches: Sketch > On the sketch plane then draws on it. Adjust current plane… changes it.
  • The plane stays where it was placed: it doesn't follow the object if you change the object later.
  • A sketch on a face keeps its back on the face: change its Height in Properties, or size it across the face with the bottom bar (Y for a front face), and it grows outward.

On a curved surface: a worked example

On a cylinder, sphere or another smooth CAD solid you draw on a flat plane touching the curved surface at the spot you choose (a tangent plane). The outline is cut or pushed straight in, not wrapped around.

Try this: insert a cylinder, set Diameter to 40 mm and Height to 40 mm, then cut a 6 mm diameter pocket into its side. Click any picture to enlarge it.

1. Choose where to sketch

Open Insert → Sketch → On a surface…. Click the side of the cylinder where you want the pocket, away from its top and bottom edges. The app calculates the surface point and the direction pointing out of it. If the model is rebuilding, wait, then click again.

2. Check the plane

A grid previews the plane. Leave Offset at 0 and leave Flip unchecked for this pocket. Choose Sketch here.

Cancel closes placement and restores the previous active plane. The plane preview itself does not cut the model or create a History step.

3. Draw the pocket outline

The normal sketch board opens. The crossing axes mark the point you picked on the cylinder. Choose Circle, click the axes' crossing, and click again to finish a rough circle. Switch to Select, click the circle, and set Diameter to 6 mm in the side panel. You can also click its diameter label to enter 6. Then choose Done.

Cancel leaves Sketch mode without creating the solid. Escape cancels the current drawing action; use the Cancel button to leave the sketch.

4. Cut inward

In Make it solid, keep Push up, choose Cut into the face, and enter 6 mm in Height. Here Height is the cut depth. Choose OK.

5. Inspect and save

Use Save to keep an editable .3dj project. The created sketch solid retains its position and orientation when reopened. Its original plane information is stored with the sketch.

Reuse a plane or make a raised boss

  • Place sketch plane… → Use plane keeps a plane active without immediately drawing. Sketch → On the sketch plane reuses it.
  • Adjust current plane… offsets or flips the active plane, starting from its current placement.
  • For a raised boss, choose New object instead of a cut. A perfectly tangent boss may only touch a cylinder at a point or line. Use a small negative offset, such as −1 mm, to give it real overlap, then select the boss and cylinder and use Object → Merge → Apply.
  • Click Workplane to return to the floor. The active plane is temporary; created sketch solids are saved.

If the result is unexpected

  • No cut: check the depth, flip direction and outline position. An outline far from the picked point may miss a curved body entirely.
  • Cannot pick: wait until the model has finished rebuilding. Near a singular tip, choose a smoother nearby point. On a rough or scanned mesh, flat areas may be broken into small pieces: click where it is flattest, or use the offset to move the plane.
  • The body changed: plane placement stays fixed in space. It does not automatically reattach after the supporting body changes. Place a new plane if needed.
  • Wanted a wrapped drawing: this tool makes a flat tangent sketch with a straight extrusion or cut. Surface wrapping and constant-depth curved engraving are not included.
