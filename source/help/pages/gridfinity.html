Gridfinity

Gridfinity is a popular, open storage system: baseplates made of a grid of 42 mm pockets, and bins (and other pieces) whose feet drop into those pockets and click together. 3DJames makes both, with the standard sizes, so they work with Gridfinity pieces from anywhere.

A baseplate and a bin

  1. Insert > Parts > Organizing > Gridfinity baseplate, then a size (for example 3 × 3).
  2. Gridfinity bin and a size. In Properties, change Units wide, Units deep and Units tall: a unit is 42 mm across and 7 mm up.
  3. To see them fit, put the bin's middle over a cell of the baseplate: type the position in Properties (the cells are 42 mm apart). Both stand on the floor, so the bin's foot sits right down in the pocket.

Print the baseplate flat, and the bin the right way up.

The numbers

Bin: Units wide / deep / tallHow many 42 mm cells, and how many 7 mm heights. A 2 × 1 × 3 bin is 83.5 × 41.5 × 21 mm.
Bin: Wall thickness1.2 mm is the usual; thicker for a sturdier bin.
Bin: Stacking lipThe rim that the foot of another bin sits in, so bins stack. It needs a bin 2 units tall or more.
Bin: Magnet holesRound holes under each cell for 6.5 × 2.4 mm magnets.
Bin: CompartmentsWalls that divide the bin across and front to back.
Baseplate: Floor under the pockets0 gives the open frame (quickest and lightest). A floor of 2.8 mm or more can have magnet holes, to match the bins'.

Good to know

  • The pockets are 0.25 mm roomier than the feet on each side, so a bin slides in without force. If yours are too tight or too loose, your printer is the reason: print a tolerance test to find its clearance.
  • The bin's cavity goes down to a 0.8 mm floor inside each foot, so a bin is not solid underneath. It has no label tab or scoop; cut or add those with the other tools.
  • A big baseplate can take a few seconds to rebuild when you change a number. Plates up to 12 × 12 units and bins up to 8 × 8 × 20 are allowed.
