Unfinished lines and changing outlines

A line doesn't have to go back to where it started. Draw part of an outline, stop, do something else, and finish it later. Only closed outlines become solid, so close them before Done makes the solid. The same works the other way round: take a side out of a finished outline and draw something new in its place.

Stop, carry on, close

  1. With Line (L) or Arc (A), click from point to point. Press Enter or Esc (or double-click) to stop. The line stays, and its two loose ends get an orange ring. The note at the top left counts the unfinished lines.
  2. To carry on, click one of the orange rings with Line or Arc and keep clicking. It is still the same line.
  3. Click the line's other orange ring (or the ringed starting point while you draw) and it closes into an outline.

While you draw, the box on the right has Stop here and Close with a line (straight back to the start). For unfinished lines it has Close it with a line and Join close ends.

Lines join up

When a line ends on the loose end of another line, the two become one line. So you can draw an outline in pieces, in any order: draw a few separate lines, then join their ends, and the last join closes the whole thing into one outline, not several small ones. This also works between sketches edited together (see Several sketches together).

Loose ends pull you in

A point you click within 2 mm of a loose end (or of where the line you are drawing started) goes exactly onto it, so lines that almost meet really join. The pointer says Loose end: joins here.

  • Hold Alt to reach loose ends from much further: 10 mm. Dashed orange circles show how far each one reaches while Alt is held.
  • Need a point just next to a loose end, not on it? Pull back: move the pointer a little way back out, away from the end. The end lets go and the point follows the pointer. Leave and come back in, and it grabs again.
  • Hold Ctrl to place a point exactly where the pointer is, with no snapping at all.
  • Dragging a loose end with Select works the same way: within 2 mm of another loose end (10 mm with Alt) they join.
  • The Alt distance can be changed on the right under Snap to (Alt: loose ends reach); Loose ends there turns this pull off.
  • Lines that nearly touch: Join close ends (right side, or in the window Done shows) moves loose ends within the Alt distance together and joins them.

Done with lines still open

Clicking Done with unfinished lines asks what to do:

  • Close with straight lines: each unfinished line gets a straight side from one loose end to the other, then Done goes on.
  • Finish without them (only when there is already a closed outline): the solid is made from the closed outlines. The open lines stay in the sketch, not solid, so you can finish them later with Edit sketch.
  • Join close ends (only when some loose ends nearly touch): moves them together and joins them, then Done goes on.
  • Keep drawing: back to the board.

Pick a side of an unfinished line and press Delete to take that side out (the line splits in two), or pick its end corner and click Delete corner to make it one side shorter.

Change part of a finished outline

An outline is closed, but one part of it is wrong? Take that part out and draw something new in its place. It works on any outline drawn with Line, Arc, Rectangle, Slot or Polygon, also later with Edit sketch.

  1. Click Select (S) and click the side you want to replace. To take out more, hold Shift and click more sides.
  2. Click Delete segment in the top bar (next to Delete), or press Delete. The outline opens up where the side was: it is now an unfinished line with two orange loose ends. The button is pale until a side is picked.
  3. Line (or Arc, if it was picked) carries straight on from one open end. Draw the new part; you can mix lines and arcs and type lengths like always.
  4. Click the other orange end. The outline is closed again and it is one shape, as before.

Stopping halfway: press Esc. The outline simply stays open (an unfinished line) until you close it, now or later. Ctrl+Z brings back the outline exactly as it was before the side was taken out (after you closed it again: press Ctrl+Z twice).

Good to know:

  • Rules and sizes on the sides you keep stay. Rules on the sides you took out are removed, and so are rules on corners that were deleted with them.
  • Sides that are not next to each other can be taken out together: the outline then falls apart into separate unfinished lines.
  • Circles and helper lines have no sides to take out: cut pieces off them with Trim instead, or delete and redraw them.
  • A frozen shape must be unfrozen first.
  • To take out only one corner (its two sides become one straight side), pick the corner and click Delete corner. To change a straight side into a curve, drag its ◆ instead.
  • To cut a side only part of the way, up to where another line crosses it, use Trim.
