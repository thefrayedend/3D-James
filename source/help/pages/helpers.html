Helpers: common jobs in a few clicks

Object > Helpers does jobs people do all the time on parts they have already made, like making a lid, joining two parts with a hinge, or cutting a part to fit the printer. Each one adds one step to History and opens it in Properties, where you can change its sizes and watch the part update. Delete the step to undo the job, or turn it off to compare.

  • Most helpers need a solid: the ones that work on a face can't go on an imported STL or anything merged with a thread. Make a lid, Fit around, Split and the hinge also work on meshes.
  • The gaps (0.15 to 0.3 mm) suit most printers. If a part comes out too tight, open its step and make the gap a little bigger.

Split to fit the printer

For a part too big for the bed, or one that prints better in two pieces. Pick it and choose Split to fit the printer. The green plane starts straight across the longest side, in the middle; move or tilt it to where the cut should go, then click Split. Both halves are kept, with pegs on one and matching holes in the other, so they line up when you glue or press them together. The split step then opens in Properties, with its connector settings.

  • Connectors: Pegs and holes; Pin holes (a hole in each half, and loose pins printed as their own object beside it); or a Dovetail (a rail on one half that slides into a slot in the other, along the longest way across the cut).
  • The connectors go where the cut face has room for them, so the walls around them stay strong. If they don't fit, make them smaller.
  • Pegs and pins are made shorter by themselves where a half is too thin for them, so a hole never comes out the far side.
  • You can also add connectors to any split: in the Split bar choose Connectors, or open a split step that keeps both halves.

Hollow out

The same as Faces > Hollow: click the faces to leave open (the top of a box), set the wall thickness and click Apply. See Edges, faces, split and emboss.

Make a lid

Pick a box and choose Make a lid. A lid appears beside it in a new colour, ready to print upside down: the plate on the bed and the lip up. Turn it over to put it on. If the box is still solid, it is hollowed out first (2 mm walls, top left open); change the walls in its Hollow step.

  • Kind: Push-on (a lip inside the box), Snap-on (a small bead on the lip clicks into a groove that is cut inside the box), or Sliding (grooves are cut in the walls, open at the front, and a flat lid slides in from the front).
  • A sliding lid needs walls thicker than the groove depth plus about 0.8 mm. 3DJames tells you if they are too thin.
  • The lid follows the box: change the box's size and the lid changes with it. Move it with Beside the box X and Y.

Hinge between two parts

Put the two parts side by side on the floor, edges facing each other, a few millimetres apart. Pick both (click one, Ctrl+click the other) and choose Hinge between two parts. They fold shut top to top, like a book closing.

  • Pin hinge: knuckles along the top of the facing edges take turns between the parts, with a pin through them. The parts stay separate objects. Set the knuckle size, how many (0 picks for you), the pin, and the clearance.
  • Living hinge: a thin strip along the bottom joins the parts into one object, and the gap between them is what bends. 0.4 to 0.8 mm thick with a 2 to 4 mm gap works well in PETG; PP bends best. PLA cracks after a few bends.
  • For a box with a flip-up lid, make the box and the lid as two open trays of the same height (the lid can be shallow, with the same walls), side by side, then add the hinge. Parts of different heights can't close flat; Properties says so.
  • If the parts overlap or aren't side by side, nothing is added and a message says what to change.

Screw holes on a face

Pick a solid, choose Screw holes on a face, choose the kind and screw size, then click on a flat face where each hole goes. Click Done when you have them all. All the holes on one face are one step.

  • Kind: plain, countersunk (flat-head screws), counterbored (socket-head screws), heat-set insert, or nut trap (a hex pocket for a nut). Sizes M2 to M8 fill in the standard sizes; you can change any of them.
  • Depth 0 goes through the wall under the face, and stops there. It doesn't go on through the far side of a hollow part.
  • Click to add more in the step adds holes on the same face. For holes on another face, start Screw holes again.

Fit around

To make a pocket that a real part fits into (a battery, a bearing, a tool, a magnet), place the part where it goes, overlapping the object. Pick the part only and choose Fit around. It is cut out of everything it passes through, with a gap all round, and the part itself stays.

  • It is like Subtract, but it leaves room for the part after printing. Untick Keep … as its own object to use the part up.
  • On an imported mesh, the gap is made by stretching the part slightly, which suits rounded and box-like parts best.

Snap clip on a face

Place the two parts where they go, with the hook's spot under the part it clicks into. Pick both (click one, Ctrl+click the other), choose Snap clip on a face and click the face where the hook stands. The hook, with a catch at its tip, stands on that face, and the window it clicks into is cut in the other part, with a gap all round.

  • With one part picked, only the hook is made: pick the part it clicks into later in the step's Clicks into. Or set a snap clip to Window to cut a window yourself (give it the hook's width, thickness and catch).
  • The hook needs to go into the other part: if it doesn't reach it, the step says so. Change the hook's length, or move the part.
  • PETG bends better than PLA for clips. Make the hook longer for an easier click, or thicker for a firmer one.

Vents on a face

Pick a solid, choose Vents on a face and click a flat face. It fills with holes inside a margin, cut through the wall under it: a honeycomb, round holes or slots. Change the hole size, the bars between them and the margin in Properties.

  • Only whole holes are cut: the pattern stays inside the margin, and around holes already in the face.
  • Holes about 3 to 6 mm wide with bars of 1.2 mm or more print cleanly without support on a top face.
