Mirror, copies and patterns

Make the other half of a symmetric part, a turned copy, or a row or circle of copies. All on the Object tab. For a plain copy beside the original, use Duplicate (Ctrl+D).

Mirror

Pick one or more objects, then Object > Mirror:

  • Flip left–right / front–back / upside down: the object itself is flipped, in place.
  • Mirror copy to the right / to the back / on top: a mirror image is added as a new, separate object on that side, touching the original. With several objects picked, they are mirrored together, across the far side of the whole selection.
  • Turned copy (¼ turn left, ½ turn, ¼ turn right): a copy turned around the up axis, placed beside the original with a 5 mm gap. Also its own object.

The copy is picked right away, so you can move, color or change it without touching the original. When the original is a sketch, the copy is a sketch too: Edit sketch opens its outline, drawn mirrored, and its height or spin can be changed. Other objects become a mirrored or turned copy that you can move, size, round or combine like any object.

Good to know: a mirror or turned copy is made from the object as it is now; changing the original later does not change the copy (use Pattern for copies that follow). A sketch made with Sweep, a part turn (Spin around less than 360°), or a position typed as a formula is copied as a mirrored object instead of an editable sketch. Projects from older versions, where a mirror copy was joined to the original, still open the same. Undo removes the copy.

Pattern: copies that follow

Pick one object and click Pattern (Object tab). The copies are part of the same object and follow it: change the original and every copy changes with it. Set it up in Properties:

  • In a row: Count (how many in all, the original included) and Step X / Y / Z: how far each copy is from the one before, in mm. For a grid, pattern the row again in the other direction.
  • Around a center: Count, Total angle (360° spreads them evenly all the way round) and Center X / Y: the point they turn around (the copies turn around an upright line through it). Bolt holes around a flange, spokes, teeth.

The pattern is a step in History: change the count or step any time. To pattern holes, pattern the hole cutter (a cylinder or a hole part) first and then subtract it.

Patterns inside a drawing (a row of holes in one outline) are made on the sketch board instead: see More sketch tools.
