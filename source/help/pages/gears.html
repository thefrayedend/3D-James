Gears

Insert tab > Parts > Motion and gears. Spur, helical and bevel gears, racks and worms, with standard involute teeth. Set the module (tooth size) and the number of teeth, and the gear is made.

Make two gears that mesh

  1. Add a Spur gear (Parts > Motion and gears > Spur gear > 30 teeth). Add another (12 teeth).
  2. Both must have the same module and the same pressure angle (1.5 and 20° from the menu).
  3. Place them so the middles are module × (teeth + teeth) ÷ 2 apart: 1.5 × (30 + 12) ÷ 2 = 31.5 mm. Type it in Properties (Placement: Position).
  4. Turn the small one half a tooth so a tooth meets a gap: 180° ÷ its teeth = 15°. (Properties > Rotation Z.)

Print them with a little room: move them apart by 0.1 to 0.2 mm so they turn freely.

The kinds

  • Spur: straight teeth. The simplest and strongest to print.
  • Helical: slanted teeth (set the helix angle, 20° is common): quieter and smoother. A helical pair needs opposite hands: one turned one way, the other the other way (use Mirror for the second). Herringbone is two opposed halves in one gear, so it needs no thrust washer; it is made as two pieces touching along the middle.
  • Bevel: teeth on a cone, for two gears whose shafts meet at 90°. Say how many teeth the other gear has (Teeth of the gear it meshes with), and the cone is made to match. Two with the same teeth make a pair; to match 16 and 32, make one of each.
  • Rack: a straight bar of teeth, for a gear of the same module to run along (turns a spin into a slide).
  • Worm: a screw-shaped gear, with 1 to 4 starts, right- or left-handed, that turns a spur or helical gear (the worm wheel) of the same module: place the gear's middle module × teeth ÷ 2 + the worm's pitch diameter ÷ 2 from the worm's middle, with its shaft at right angles.

Numbers

  • Module: the tooth size (the pitch diameter divided by the teeth). 1 to 2 suits most printers.
  • Pressure angle: 20° is the standard.
  • Shaft hole and a flat on it for a D shaft (a stepper motor's 5 mm shaft has a 0.5 mm flat); a hub adds a collar to put a set screw in.
  • Too few teeth make pointed teeth; the part says so. Gears with fewer than about 10 teeth are weak.

Limits

The teeth are drawn as short straight pieces (many more on a big gear), which is fine for printing. There is no backlash setting: leave a little distance between gears, as above. The bevel gear and the worm are close approximations of the true shapes, good for light work and for learning, not for precision machinery.
